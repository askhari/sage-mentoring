- Common practice for building a colorant database is to prepare tint ladders: each colorant mixed with white at five or more concentrations (abstract) → tints-shades-tones
- Two-constant Kubelka-Munk theory characterizes each colorant by a unit absorption coefficient and a unit scattering coefficient (two constants per colorant) (abstract) → pigment-mixing-behavior
- These per-colorant coefficients are solved by least-squares fitting over all samples in the tint ladder, which is why the white paint is part of the characterization (abstract) → pigment-mixing-behavior
- The study's goal was the minimum number of samples needed to characterize each colorant for the database, tested on artist acrylic and oil paints (abstract) → target-color-mixing
- For pigment identification (qualitative, concentration unknown), a masstone (pure pigment) plus one mixture with white at unknown concentration suffices (abstract) → target-color-mixing
- For pigment mapping (quantitative, estimating concentrations), a masstone plus one mixture with white of known concentration suffices; best at about 40-60% pigment in white (abstract) → target-color-mixing
- Two samples per colorant replace the full tint ladder, greatly simplifying colorant-database construction (abstract) → target-color-mixing
- The masstone-plus-one-tint pair is enough, implying a single mid-concentration tint carries enough information on the colorant's scattering and absorption relative to white (inference from abstract, not stated explicitly) ⚠️ → tints-shades-tones
