## Afterimages
- Looking at a coloured surface produces a later (negative) afterimage in a colour family approximately complementary to the one viewed; Hering used this phenomenon for his opponent theory (Introduction) → afterimages
- Standard explanations are photoreceptor/retinal adaptation and physiological wavelength opponency from retina onwards; the authors say the match between cell tuning and perceived opponency is too loose, at least up to V1 (Introduction) → afterimages
- Question tested: is the afterimage colour due to adaptation to the dominant wavelength reflected by a patch in a multicoloured scene, or does it depend on the patch's perceived colour? E.g. a green-looking surface reflecting more red light would give a green afterimage under adaptation, a red one under the perceived-colour view (Introduction) → afterimages
- Finding: afterimage colour, like perceived colour, depends on the ratio of light of different wavebands from the patch and its surrounds, not on the patch's wavelength-energy composition alone; the authors conclude that retinal adaptation and physiological opponency are inadequate and that the afterimage is generated after colours are generated in the brain (Abstract; Discussion) → afterimages
- Method: four Land Mondrians (A-D) of matte Color Aid papers, central patch green, yellow, magenta, blue; lit by three 350 W projectors (long/middle/short-wave gelatin filters, separate rheostats); central patches were made to reflect similar wavelength-energy composition while keeping their colour; 30 s fixation, verbal naming (blue/yellow/red/green), then closest of 10 Munsell chips (value 6, chroma 8); 3 repeats x 4 displays x 6 observers = 72 trials; observers passed the Farnsworth-Munsell 100 Hue test (Materials and Methods; Fig. 1) → afterimages
- Results: opponent afterimages in 86.11% of trials, non-opponent 12.50%, none 1.39% (Fig. 2); by display green 94%, yellow 100%, magenta 100%, blue 56% (blue not significant, t(5)=0.27, p>0.05) (Results; Fig. 3) → afterimages
- Munsell matches, most frequent: green patch → red 5R (72%); yellow → purple-blue 5PB (78%); magenta → 5G and 5BG (39% each); blue → yellow-red 5YR (89%) (Results; Fig. 4) → afterimages
- Worked example for display A: cone-adaptation reasoning predicts a greenish afterimage (L cones more stimulated), but observers reported red, so the afterimage is built after receptor level, after colour is generated in cortex (Discussion) → afterimages
- Retinal von Kries gain control judged unlikely: the dominant waveband was the same in centre and surround up to 10°, afterimages are opponent to perceived colour not wavelength, and horizontal cells/V1 cells span only about 1-2° (Discussion) → afterimages
- Limits: 6 observers (5 male), 4 displays, 3 repeats; blue display gave weaker, more ambiguous afterimages (authors suggest lower short-wave spatial resolution and fewer S cones, reasons unclear); cortical site is argued, not tested here (V4 "likely", V1/V2 may cooperate) (Discussion) → afterimages
- Authors say naming plus Munsell matching was used to cope with the fluctuating nature of afterimages (Methods, Testing) → afterimages
- ⚠️ Internal inconsistency: the Discussion states "94% for red, 100% for blue, 100% for green and over 55% for yellow", which does not match the Results (green 94%, yellow and magenta 100%, blue 56%); trust Results/Fig. 3 (Discussion vs Results) → afterimages

## Color constancy
- Definition: ability to discount the wavelength-energy composition of the illuminating light and assign a constant colour to a surface; the authors mean a constant colour category, not an identical hue, since hue shifts (lighter/darker) with the light (Introduction) → color-constancy
- Link with afterimages: central patches kept their colour category while reflecting similar wavelength compositions; the afterimage followed the perceived colour, so afterimage colour is "constant" like surface colour and is formed by spatial ratio-taking (Introduction; Color and Wavelength) → color-constancy
- A patch seen in "void" mode (isolated from surrounds) would appear white or light grey and its afterimage a darker neutral grey (Methods, Adjustments) → color-constancy
- Cortical involvement: late-stage cortical mechanism; V4 likely critical (supported by an imaging study of afterimages, Sakai et al.), with V1/V2 cooperating; V1 wavelength-opponent cells follow wavelength composition rather than perceived colour (Discussion) → color-constancy
- Authors side with earlier work questioning von Kries/cone contrast as a full account of constancy, giving higher cortical mechanisms a larger role (Discussion) → color-constancy

## Simultaneous contrast / induction controls
- Design departs from Anstis et al. (1978), who used uniform monochromatic patches/surrounds (and grey spots on coloured surrounds) to study whether afterimages depend on simultaneous colour contrast/induction; here natural multi-waveband Mondrians were used (Introduction) → simultaneous-contrast
- Surrounds extended more than 10° from the centre and were in the complementary colour family; 10° chosen as the range beyond which surround modulation of colour declines rapidly (Methods, Fig. 1) → simultaneous-contrast
- Control: when a patch reflected more of a waveband, its surrounds were chosen to reflect more of the same waveband, to avoid induction effects (Methods, Testing) → simultaneous-contrast
- Control in display C: the surround was the same green as the predicted afterimage of the magenta centre, so the afterimage could not be explained by colour induction from the surround (Methods, Multicolored Displays) → simultaneous-contrast
