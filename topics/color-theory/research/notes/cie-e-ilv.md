## light-spectrum
- Visible radiation (17-21-003): optical radiation that can directly produce a visual sensation. It has no sharp spectral limits (they depend on the flux reaching the retina and observer sensitivity); the lower limit is generally taken between 360 and 400 nm and the upper between 760 and 830 nm. → light-spectrum
- Light, psychophysical (17-21-012): radiation considered by its ability to excite the visual system. Note: using "light" for non-visible optical radiation is not recommended by the CIE. → light-spectrum
- Light, photometric (17-21-013): radiation within the spectral range of visible radiation. Note: in physics "light" is sometimes used for all optical radiation (about 100 nm to 1 mm); the CIE advises against this. → light-spectrum
- Spectrum (17-21-015): display or specification of the monochromatic components of the radiation considered; can be line, continuous, or mixed. → light-spectrum
- Wavelength (17-21-025): distance, along the propagation direction of a periodic wave, between two successive points of equal phase; equals phase velocity / frequency; nm and µm are common for optical radiation. → light-spectrum
- Spectral distribution (17-21-029): the density of a radiant/luminous/photon quantity with respect to wavelength (e.g. W·nm⁻¹ for radiant flux). → light-spectrum
- Relative spectral distribution (17-21-030): spectral distribution divided by a fixed reference value (average, maximum or arbitrary value of that distribution); dimensionless. → light-spectrum
- ⚠️ "Spectral power distribution" as such is not a headword; the closest e-ILV terms are 17-21-029 / 17-21-030 (and "illuminant", 17-23-018, defined via relative spectral power distribution). → light-spectrum

## light-matter-interaction
- Reflection (17-24-047): a surface or medium returns radiation without changing the frequency of its monochromatic components; includes surface reflection and volume reflection (scattered back from the interior). → light-matter-interaction
- Transmission (17-24-048): passage of radiation through a medium without change of frequency of its monochromatic components. → light-matter-interaction
- Scattering, also called diffusion (17-24-049): spatial redistribution of a beam deflected in many directions by a surface or medium, with no frequency change. Can be selective (depends on wavelength) or non-selective (does not). → light-matter-interaction
- Diffuse reflection (17-24-054): scattering by reflection where, at macroscopic scale, there is no regular (specular) reflection. → light-matter-interaction
- Absorption (17-24-081): the e-ILV page only refers to IEV 705-02-07 for the definition (no own text). → light-matter-interaction
- Reflectance (17-24-064, symbol ρ): ratio of reflected to incident radiant flux; may be spectral (function of wavelength); dimensionless. By energy conservation absorptance + reflectance + transmittance = 1 (except with polarized radiation); total reflectance = regular + diffuse reflectance. → light-matter-interaction
- Reflectance factor (17-24-070, symbol R): ratio of flux reflected by a surface element into a given cone to that reflected into the same directions by a perfect reflecting diffuser under identical illumination; the spectral version is R(λ); can exceed 1 for specular surfaces; tends to reflectance as the cone approaches 2π sr. → light-matter-interaction

## photoreceptors
- Cones (17-22-002): photoreceptors in the retina with light-sensitive pigments able to start photopic vision. → photoreceptors
- Rods (17-22-003): retinal photoreceptors with a pigment that starts scotopic vision. → photoreceptors
- Photopic vision (17-22-016): vision by the normal eye with cones as principal active photoreceptors; normally at adaptation luminance of at least about 5 cd·m⁻²; colour perception is typical of it. → photoreceptors
- Scotopic vision (17-22-017): vision with rods as principal active photoreceptors; typically below about 0.005 cd·m⁻²; no colour perception and sensitivity shifted toward shorter wavelengths relative to photopic. → photoreceptors
- Mesopic vision (17-22-018): vision intermediate between photopic and scotopic; both cones and rods are functional. → photoreceptors
- Adaptation (17-22-012): process by which the state of the visual system is modified by prior and current exposure to stimuli differing in luminance, spectral distribution and angular subtense. → photoreceptors
- Chromatic adaptation (17-22-013): visual process by which the eye approximately compensates for changes in the colours of stimuli, especially when the illuminant changes. → illuminant-color

## trichromacy
- Colour stimulus (17-23-002): visible radiation entering the eye and producing a sensation of chromatic or achromatic colour. → trichromacy
- Colour, psychophysical (17-23-001): specification of a colour stimulus by operationally defined values such as three tristimulus values. → trichromacy
- Colour matching (17-23-031): making a colour stimulus appear the same in colour as a given one. → trichromacy
- Trichromatic system (17-23-036): system for specifying colour stimuli by tristimulus values, based on matching by additive mixture of three suitably chosen reference colour stimuli. → trichromacy
- Tristimulus values (17-23-038): the amounts of the reference colour stimuli, in a given trichromatic system, required to match the stimulus; in CIE systems written R,G,B; X,Y,Z; or X10,Y10,Z10 etc. → trichromacy
- Colour-matching functions (17-23-039): tristimulus values of monochromatic stimuli of equal radiant flux; used to compute tristimulus values of a stimulus from its colour stimulus function. → trichromacy

## opponent-process
- No term "opponent colours" or "opponent process" found in the term lists of sections 17-21 to 17-24 (searched by headword list). Not covered (opponent-process).
- Only related: unique hue (17-22-068): a hue not describable by hue names other than its own; four unique hues (red, green, yellow, blue) forming two opposing pairs, red-green and yellow-blue (Note 1); also called elementary hue. → opponent-process, hue

## color-vision-deficiency
- Defective colour vision (17-22-023): anomaly of vision with reduced ability to discriminate between some or all colours; main types listed: anomalous trichromatism (deuteranomaly, protanomaly, tritanomaly), dichromatism (deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia), monochromatism. → color-vision-deficiency
- Dichromatism (17-22-027): defective colour vision in which all colours can be matched by additive mixtures of only two matching stimuli. → color-vision-deficiency
- Anomalous trichromatism (17-22-035): a form of trichromatism in which colour discrimination is less than normal. → color-vision-deficiency
- Protanopia (17-22-031): dichromatic defective colour vision in which reddish and greenish aspects cannot be distinguished and reddish colours look abnormally dark (page read via summary; wording paraphrased). → color-vision-deficiency
- Deuteranopia (17-22-026): dichromatic defective colour vision with reddish/greenish discrimination absent, without abnormal darkening of colours (paraphrased). → color-vision-deficiency
- Tritanopia (17-22-034): dichromatic defective colour vision in which discrimination of bluish and yellowish contents of colours is absent. → color-vision-deficiency
- Other terms exist in the list: deutan 17-22-024, deuteranomaly 17-22-025, protan 17-22-029, protanomaly 17-22-030, tritan 17-22-032, tritanomaly 17-22-033, monochromatism 17-22-028, pseudoisochromatic plates 17-22-120 (not read). → color-vision-deficiency

## illuminant-color
- Illuminant (17-23-018): radiation with a defined relative spectral power distribution, specified over the wavelength range relevant to perceived object colour. Note: outside technical use, "illuminant" often means any light falling on a body or scene. → illuminant-color
- Source of optical radiation: term exists as 17-21-032 (not read); ⚠️ the "illuminant vs light source" contrast is therefore only implied by 17-23-018 being a defined spectral distribution, not stated explicitly. → illuminant-color
- CIE standard illuminant (17-23-021): an illuminant standardized by the CIE for harmonization. Illuminant A: relative spectral distribution of Planckian radiation at about 2 855,5 K. Illuminant D65: represents a phase of daylight with correlated colour temperature about 6 500 K. Should be used where appropriate for comparable results; B, C and other daylight illuminants are no longer "standard" but "CIE illuminants". → illuminant-color
- CIE illuminant (17-23-019): illuminant as defined by the CIE; A and D65 are also CIE standard illuminants. → illuminant-color
- Planckian radiator (17-24-004, also blackbody): ideal thermal radiator that completely absorbs all incident radiation regardless of wavelength, direction or polarization; at a given temperature it emits the maximum spectral radiance at every wavelength and direction. → illuminant-color
- Colour temperature (17-23-067, Tc): temperature of a Planckian radiator whose radiation has the same chromaticity as the given stimulus; unit kelvin. → illuminant-color
- Correlated colour temperature (17-23-068, CCT/Tcp): temperature of the Planckian radiator whose chromaticity is nearest to that of the given spectral distribution, evaluated on the (1976 modified) u′,v′ diagram; not to be used if the source chromaticity is too far from the Planckian locus. → illuminant-color
- Chromatic adaptation (17-22-013): see above. → illuminant-color

## metamerism
- Metamerism (17-23-006): property of spectrally different colour stimuli that have the same tristimulus values in a specified colorimetric system. → metamerism
- Metameric colour stimuli / metamers (17-23-008): spectrally different colour stimuli with the same tristimulus values in a specified colorimetric system; the property is metamerism. → metamerism
- Metamerism index (17-23-007): degree to which two colours that match under a reference illuminant (observer) differ when a test illuminant (observer) with different spectral composition or responsivity is substituted; expressed as a colour difference, computed with a stated CIE colour-difference formula. → metamerism

## hue
- Hue (17-22-067): attribute of visual perception by which an area looks like one of red, yellow, green or blue, or a blend of adjacent pairs of these arranged in a closed ring. → hue
- Perceived colour (17-22-040): characteristic of visual perception describable by hue, brightness (or lightness) and colourfulness (or saturation or chroma); depends on spectrum, size, shape, structure, surround, adaptation and experience of the observer. → hue, value, chroma, saturation-and-colorfulness
- CIELAB hue angle: h_ab = arctan(b*/a*) (17-23-076, Note 1). → hue, cielab-basics

## value
(e-ILV has no "value" in the Munsell sense; the equivalents are brightness and lightness.)
- Brightness (17-22-059): attribute of visual perception by which an area appears to emit, transmit or reflect more or less light; not restricted to primary light sources. → value
- Lightness, of a related colour (17-22-063): brightness of an area judged relative to a similarly illuminated area that appears white or highly transmitting; only related colours have lightness. → value
- CIELAB L* approximates lightness (17-23-076, Note 1). → value, cielab-basics

## chroma
- Chroma (17-22-074): colourfulness of an area judged in proportion to the brightness of a similarly illuminated area that appears grey, white or highly transmitting. Note: under set conditions at photopic levels, chroma of a related colour stays roughly constant over illuminance levels except at very high brightness. → chroma

## saturation-and-colorfulness
- Colourfulness (17-22-072): attribute of visual perception by which the perceived colour of an area appears more or less chromatic. Note: for a given chromaticity, it generally increases with luminance, except at very high brightness. → saturation-and-colorfulness
- Saturation (17-22-073): colourfulness of an area judged in proportion to its own brightness. Note: at photopic levels with fixed chromaticity it stays roughly constant as luminance changes, except at very high brightness. → saturation-and-colorfulness
- Chroma (17-22-074): see above (judged relative to the brightness of a similarly illuminated reference white/grey area, not to the area's own brightness). → saturation-and-colorfulness

## cielab-basics
- CIE 1976 L*a*b* colour space (CIELAB) (17-23-076): approximately perceptually uniform three-dimensional colour space with rectangular coordinates L*, a*, b* computed from tristimulus values X, Y, Z relative to those of a specified white (Xn, Yn, Zn). L* = 116 f(Y/Yn) − 16; a* = 500[f(X/Xn) − f(Y/Yn)]; b* = 200[f(Y/Yn) − f(Z/Zn)]; f is the cube root above (6/29)³ and linear below. Note 1: L* approximates lightness, chroma is derived from a* and b*, hue angle h_ab = arctan(b*/a*). → cielab-basics
- CIE 1976 L*a*b* colour difference (17-23-077): Euclidean distance between the two points in CIELAB space. → cielab-basics
- Other related terms (not read): colour space 17-23-041, uniform colour space 17-23-071, white point 17-23-069/070, CIE 1976 L*u*v* 17-23-074. → cielab-basics

## simultaneous-contrast
- Chromatic induction (17-22-075): change in visual response when two colour stimuli are viewed side by side, each altering the other's appearance; practically instantaneous, unlike the slow chromatic adaptation. → simultaneous-contrast
- Contrast, perceptual (17-22-089): assessment of the difference in appearance of two or more parts of a field seen simultaneously or successively; examples listed include brightness contrast, lightness contrast, colour contrast, simultaneous contrast, successive contrast. → simultaneous-contrast
