## Colour meaning
- The book is a history of colour in Western culture from the Greeks to the late 20th century, covering both practice and meaning (subtitle; Google Books summary; publisher blurb). → color-meaning
- The publisher blurb says it covers Greek colour theories, Roman and Renaissance attempts to systematize colour or give it symbolic power, religious and heraldic uses of colour, and the Renaissance link with alchemy. → color-meaning
- A dedicated chapter, "Colour-Language, Colour-Symbols" (starts p. 79; ch. 5 in the Little, Brown edition record), deals with colour naming and symbolism; its content could not be read. → color-meaning
- The book's thematic chapters (e.g. Light from the East, p. 39; A Dionysian Aesthetic, p. 69) show that the discussion is organized by period and tradition, but the claims within were not accessible. → color-meaning
- Review of Gage's later "Color in Art" by John Klein (caareviews.org, 2012): Gage's books treat colour's symbolic and practical roles through history, and Gage argues in "Color and Meaning" that art history is the discipline best suited to understand colour. ⚠️ This concerns the later books, not the 1993 text. → color-meaning
- Barry Schwabsky (caareviews.org, 2000), reviewing "Color and Meaning" (1999): 19th-century German colour theory rested largely on symbolism and "moral associations"; he calls that book extended footnotes to "Colour and Culture". ⚠️ Not a statement about the 1993 book's thesis. → color-meaning

## Colour schemes / harmony history
- Chapters relevant to the history of colour theory used by artists: "The Reign of Newton" (p. 153), "Mother of All Colours" (p. 177), "Goethe's Legacy" (p. 191), "The Substance of Colour" (p. 213), "The Role of Abstraction" (p. 247) (Google Books TOC). Chevreul and harmony are not named in any chapter title. → color-schemes
- The publisher blurb mentions Newton's spectrum analysis, Goethe's colour theory, and attempts to link colour with music or treat it as an abstract language (the Perlego listing also names a chapter "The Sound of Colour"). → color-schemes
- A review on the Gombrich archive site (snippet via search, not read in full) says Gage is fully conversant with the theory of colour contrasts codified, but not discovered, by Chevreul, and that the book's disjunctive arrangement makes themes hard to grasp. ⚠️ Secondhand via a search summary; reviewer and venue not identified. → color-schemes
- A CAA review of "Color and Meaning" (via search summary) describes "Colour and Culture" as dense, ambitious and readable, and says Gage attends more to writings about colour than to examples of practice. ⚠️ Secondhand via a search summary. → color-schemes
