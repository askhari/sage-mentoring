Accessed: abstract only (Annual Reviews page and PubMed record, via search snippets and a page summary). The full text (author copy at mvr.mcgill.ca) refused the connection. No section outline was visible. Claims come from the abstract and MeSH terms and carry no page numbers; the whole article is pp. 143-166.

- The review's premise is that how an object or surface looks depends strongly on the light from neighboring objects and surfaces (abstract) → color-constancy
- It deals with complex scenes, meaning several colors in view at once or in succession, as in natural viewing (abstract) → color-constancy
- Property 1 separating a complex scene from an isolated light patch is that an object's color is not fully determined by the light it sends to the eye (abstract) → color-constancy
- Property 2 is that a scene's color representation affects more than hue, saturation and brightness. It also affects form, texture and object segmentation (abstract) → color-constancy
- Color constancy is explicitly among the topics covered, along with chromatic contributions to orientation, contour, depth and motion perception (abstract) → color-constancy
- Color interacts with changing spatial and temporal context. The MeSH term "Adaptation, Physiological" is listed, which suggests adaptation is part of the treatment (PubMed MeSH terms; inference from the term only) → color-constancy

Not covered: simultaneous-contrast (chromatic induction), assimilation, afterimages and equiluminance. The abstract does not mention them. The article body very likely does, but I could not verify that.
Level: university/specialist review (reliability B as given).
