## Scope and level
- The publisher blurb describes the book as a resource for professionals in imaging, entertainment, materials, design, architecture and lighting. It also says the book can serve as a text for senior undergraduate and postgraduate courses. It covers the fundamentals and phenomena of color appearance, plus techniques to measure, model and predict it (Perlego blurb) → cielab-basics
- The 3rd edition has 21 chapters. It adds a final chapter on high-dynamic-range color space that the 2nd edition lacked (TOC; the SPIE review snippet also mentions this) → cielab-basics

## Domain light-and-vision (locations only)
- Ch. 1 "Human Color Vision" has these sections: 1.1 Optics of the Eye, 1.2 The Retina, 1.3 Visual Signal Processing, 1.4 Mechanisms of Color Vision, 1.5 Spatial and Temporal Properties, 1.6 Color Vision Deficiencies, 1.7 Key Features for Color Appearance Modeling (TOC, ch. 1) → photoreceptors
- Photoreceptors are treated in the retina section (§1.2). Trichromacy and opponent processing are presumably in §1.3 to §1.4, but this is inferred from the section titles and not confirmed from the text (TOC, ch. 1) → photoreceptors
- Color vision deficiencies have their own section, §1.6 (TOC, ch. 1) → color-vision-deficiency
- Ch. 3 "Colorimetry", §3.3 "Light Sources and Illuminants", is where the book treats light sources and standard illuminants. §3.4 covers Colored Materials, §3.5 The Human Visual Response, §3.6 Tristimulus Values and Color Matching Functions (TOC, ch. 3) → illuminant-color
- No section on correlated color temperature (CCT) appears in the TOC. Its treatment, if any, would sit inside §3.3 and is unverified → illuminant-color
- No dedicated chapter or section on metamerism's definition appears in the TOC. Metamerism shows up in ch. 18: §18.3 "Indices of Metamerism" and §18.5 "What About Observer Metamerism?" (TOC, ch. 18). So the distinction between illuminant metamerism and observer metamerism is at least discussed there, and §3.2 "Why is Color?" is the likely place for the basic idea (inferred) → metamerism

## Domain color-attributes
- Ch. 4 "Color Appearance Terminology" has sections 4.1 Importance of Definitions, 4.2 Color, 4.3 Hue, 4.4 Brightness and Lightness, 4.5 Colorfulness and Chroma, 4.6 Saturation, 4.7 Unrelated and Related Colors, 4.8 Definitions in Equations, 4.9 Brightness-Colorfulness vs. Lightness-Chroma. The chapter starts on p. 85; the page numbers of the sections are approximately: 4.2 p. 86, 4.3 p. 88, 4.4 p. 88, 4.5 p. 90, 4.6 p. 91 (TOC with pages, via a search digest of a catalogue listing) → hue, value, chroma, saturation-and-colorfulness
- The book treats brightness together with lightness (§4.4) and colorfulness together with chroma (§4.5). Saturation has its own section (§4.6) (TOC, ch. 4) → value, chroma, saturation-and-colorfulness
- The book deals separately with related and unrelated colors (§4.7). §4.9 compares the brightness-colorfulness pair with the lightness-chroma pair (TOC, ch. 4) → saturation-and-colorfulness, value
- Ch. 5 "Color Order Systems" has these sections: 5.1 Overview and Requirements, 5.2 The Munsell Book of Color, 5.3 The Swedish NCS, 5.4 The Colorcurve System, 5.5 Other Color Order Systems, 5.6 Uses of Color Order Systems, 5.7 Color Naming Systems (TOC, ch. 5). The Munsell system is the first one discussed, in §5.2 → munsell-system
- Ch. 3 "Colorimetry" has §3.8 "CIE Color Spaces" and §3.9 "Color Difference Specification" (TOC, ch. 3). Ch. 10 "Color Appearance Models" has §10.3 "CIELAB", §10.4 "Why Not Use Just CIELAB?" and §10.5 "What About CIELUV?" (TOC, ch. 10). Ch. 10 presents CIELAB as a simple example of a color appearance model, and §10.4 discusses its limitations (TOC, ch. 10; the catalogue snippet agrees) → cielab-basics
- Ch. 17 has §17.7 "Munsell in Color Appearance Spaces", which relates Munsell data to appearance spaces (TOC, ch. 17) → munsell-system
