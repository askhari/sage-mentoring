- Method is experiential: the book pairs the text with about sixty colour studies (Yale page; 64 colour plates, 16 line drawings, 208 pp.), and teaching is by direct perception, not fixed colour systems (Yale page) → judging-color
- Practice comes before theory; the aim is to train observation. Albers is quoted as saying "I have taught seeing" (Newsweek 2013, app article) → judging-color
- The exercises use coloured paper, not paint; the book includes a section on why paper is used (Miami listing, TOC summary) → judging-color
- The original edition used colour flaps that can be pulled back to show how relationships change; the app adds blank templates and a colour wheel for doing the exercises (Newsweek 2013) → judging-color
- Core idea: colour is perceived relative to its neighbours, so the same colour looks different depending on its surroundings (Newsweek 2013; Yale page names "color relativity") → simultaneous-contrast
- The app description (via search summary of the app/press coverage) lists making one colour look like two, or two colours look like one, among its topics. Only a search-engine paraphrase; the exact wording is unverified → simultaneous-contrast
- The Miami TOC summary groups "color deception, such as after-images and simultaneous contrast" in one section; library TOC snippets for older editions show the heading "Why color deception?" (Miami listing; catalogue snippets) → simultaneous-contrast
- After-images are treated in the same section as simultaneous contrast (colour deception). The 2006/2013 TOC also has a heading "Optical mixture, after-image revised" next to the Bezold chapter (Miami listing; catalogue snippets, 2006/2013 TOC) → afterimages
- The book has a dedicated chapter called "The Bezold Effect" in the 1975, 2006 and 2013 editions (catalogue TOC snippets). Its content is not described in the accessible material → assimilation
- Bezold-effect and optical-mixture material sit together near the section on colour boundaries (Miami listing, TOC) → optical-mixing
- Optical mixture appears as a titled TOC section ("Optical mixture") of the book (Miami listing; catalogue snippets) → optical-mixing
- "Vibrating boundaries: enforced contours" is a chapter near the end of the book, and "vanishing boundaries" is mentioned alongside it. The Yale page names "vibrating and vanishing boundaries" as principles demonstrated by the studies (Yale page; catalogue TOC snippets) → equiluminance
- ⚠️ The accessible material never says that the vibrating-boundaries chapter involves colours of equal light intensity. The link to equiluminance comes from the caller's brief and is unverified from the accessible material → equiluminance
- The first chapter is "Color recollection: visual memory" in the 2006 and 2013 editions, and the opening part also covers "color reading" (catalogue TOC snippets; Miami listing). Its argument is not described in the accessible material → color-categories
- Light intensity and brightness are listed as topics, and the TOC has relativity of light/dark intensity and gradation studies. The Weber-Fechner Law also has a section (Newsweek 2013; Miami listing, TOC) → value-structure
- Colour temperature has a TOC section and is named among the principles on the Yale page (Yale page; Miami listing) → color-temperature
