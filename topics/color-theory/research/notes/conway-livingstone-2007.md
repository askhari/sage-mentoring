## Gaze and contrast
- Eye fixations are not random: to a first approximation they land on regions of high local contrast (paragraph on eye movements, refs 2-3) → judging-color
- Leonards et al. argued that gold in Duccio's Annunciation draws the gaze under candlelight; the authors suggest the cause may be low-level salience (local contrast created by candlelight on the gold lines) rather than symbolism (Fig. 1 paragraph) → simultaneous-contrast
- ⚠️ This is about luminance/local contrast and gaze, not chromatic induction; only a loose fit for simultaneous-contrast.

## Shadows and "alternative physics"
- Following Cavanagh, pictures can break real-world physics (shadows, reflections, transparency, perspective) without looking wrong; depth-from-shading cues need not be consistent across the whole image (Fig. 6 paragraph) → light-and-shadow-color
- Painted shadows are often physically impossible for the lighting shown, yet they read as shadows (Fig. 6 paragraph, refs 28-29) → light-and-shadow-color
- To be read as a shadow a region only needs to be relatively darker and relatively desaturated locally; its hue can be anything that meets those rules, as the Fauvists showed and Kingdom et al. confirmed (Fig. 6 paragraph, refs 30-31) → light-and-shadow-color
- Shadows change as daylight changes, which they offer as the reason for inconsistent shadows in Impressionist paintings made outdoors over time (paragraph after Fig. 6) → light-and-shadow-color

## Knowledge vs what reaches the eye
- Ordinary children draw a circle seen from the side as rounder than its true elliptical projection: they impose their knowledge of the object's 3-D shape instead of the projection on the retina (paragraph on autism and drawing, ref 14) → local-vs-perceived-color
- Mental representations (what we know about objects) can bias even drawings made from direct observation, which the authors attribute to top-down feedback in the visual system (paragraph on salience and representation) → local-vs-perceived-color, judging-color
- ⚠️ These two claims are about shape, not colour; use them for local-vs-perceived-color only as an analogy.
