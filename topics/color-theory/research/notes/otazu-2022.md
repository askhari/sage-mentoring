## Definitions and contrast vs assimilation (Introduction)
- Color induction = the perceived colour of a target is influenced by surrounding colours; artists have exploited it for centuries (Intro, para 1) → assimilation
- Two types by direction of shift: contrast = target shifts away from the colour of the nearest surround (inducer); assimilation = target shifts toward the inducer's colour (Intro, para 1) → assimilation
- Example: a grey target on a green inducer seen as reddish (opponent colour) = contrast; seen as greenish = assimilation (Intro, para 1) → simultaneous-contrast
- Which one occurs depends mainly on the spatio-chromatic properties of target and inducers; striped surrounds usually give assimilation, uniform surrounds usually give contrast (Intro, para 1) → assimilation
- Von Bezold (1876) is cited in the references for the art-related phenomenon; the paper does not use the term "Bezold effect" in the text (References) → assimilation

## Spatial frequency and stripe width (Discussion)
- Several authors concluded stripe spatial frequency is key for assimilation, and that thinner and thinner stripes produce a transition from contrast to assimilation (Discussion, para 1) → assimilation
- A cited study found chromatic assimilation maximal for rings at 3.3 cycles/degree; this study used about 2 cpd (Discussion, "Some properties of visual stimuli...") → assimilation
- Stripes alone do not guarantee assimilation: even with striped surrounds, some conditions (e.g. -l at negative luminance differences, -l/-s) gave contrast (Results, post hoc l axis) → simultaneous-contrast

## Proposed mechanisms (Introduction)
- Many authors attribute induction to neural mechanisms, probably lateral connections in primary visual cortex V1 (Intro, para 3) → assimilation
- V1 cell types: single-opponent (low-pass, <0.5 cpd, colour cells), double-opponent (band-pass 0.5-10 cpd, peak about 2 cpd, colour and luminance borders; thought to matter most for colour appearance), non-opponent (luminance cells) (Intro, para 3) → assimilation
- "Mutual inhibition" mechanism: as luminance contrast increases, colour response decreases via connections between luminance- and colour-sensitive cells (Intro, para 4) → assimilation
- Authors' interpretation assumes a cortical site, but note that effects depend on complex combinations of l, s, Y and on particular colour directions, so they speculate high-level or top-down influences; left as an open question (Discussion, para 6) → assimilation

## Stimuli and method (Methods)
- Stimulus: 11 concentric rings, each 15.5 arcmin wide, achromatic test ring (equal-energy white, l=0.66, s=0.98) among rings of opposite chromaticities; inducer luminance 20 cd/m2; test luminance differences dY = -10, -5, 0, +5, +10 cd/m2 (Methods, Stimuli, Fig. 1) → assimilation
- Display 22.6 x 16.95 deg at 134 cm, dark room; 11 participants (Methods, Apparatus, Participants) → assimilation
- Task: asymmetric matching of a comparison ring to the test ring (Methods, Experimental procedure) → assimilation
- Metric dCi = (comparison - test)/(inducer - test) along an axis: positive beyond JND = assimilation, negative beyond JND = contrast; JND estimated as dE=1 in CIELab (Methods, Statistical analysis) → assimilation
- Monnier-Shevell ring stimuli are cited among related work (Monnier & Shevell 2003/2004 in references); the paper's own stimulus is concentric rings with about 2 cpd (Discussion) → assimilation

## Findings: luminance vs chromatic channels
- Earlier study of the same group: equiluminant stimuli gave no (or negligible) assimilation; along the s axis induction went from none/weak contrast to assimilation as the luminance difference grew (Intro, para 2) → assimilation
- Abstract: assimilation appears only when the magnocellular (luminance) pathway is active; it is mainly driven by the koniocellular (s axis) not the parvocellular (l axis) channel (Abstract) → assimilation
- Hypothesis of this study (assimilation at equiluminance when both l and s chromatic channels are active, via chromatic-chromatic mutual inhibition) was NOT supported (Results, final para before "Brightness induction"; Discussion, para 3; Conclusions) → assimilation
- Assimilation was found only where luminance differences existed, e.g. dCl>0 in -l/+s at +10 and +l/-s at +10; dCs>0 in -l/+s at -10, +s at -10, +l/+s at +10 (Discussion, para 3) → assimilation
- Linear mixed models: induction on both axes depends on the l x s x Y interaction; l axis: ConditionL F(1,265.91)=33.12, ConditionY F(4,265.76)=39.56, both p<.0001; s axis: ConditionY F(4,266.91)=17.91, triple interaction F(8,266.44)=6.57, p<.0001 (Results; Appendix C, Table 2) → assimilation
- Strongest assimilation along s occurs at +s/-10 and -s/+10, when the l channel is not active; it drops dramatically when l is also active (e.g. +s/-10 vs -l/+s/-10 t=-5.36, padj<.0001) (Results, post hoc s axis) → assimilation
- Assimilation depends on the polarity of the luminance difference; weak assimilation for +l at positive dY; earlier work (cited) found assimilation when the target was brighter than the inducers (Discussion, para 2) → assimilation
- Conclusion: luminance difference is a key factor; chromatic channels influence each other via the luminance pathway; parvo -> konio influence (through magno) is stronger than the reverse; assimilation is stronger along s than l (Conclusions) → assimilation
- With all three pathways active, chromatic induction is greatly decreased (Abstract) → assimilation
- Size: effects are expressed as dC relative to JND; no raw percent magnitudes reported in text; participant random effect explained 16.8% (l) and 18.0% (s) of residual variance, AIC l=-818.40, s=-463.34 (Results) → assimilation
- Related watercolor-effect literature suggests assimilation there is luminance-dependent (Discussion, para 2) → assimilation

## Contrast notes
- Contrast was seen mainly in the -l condition (reddish-green direction; speculated to relate to foliage colours) (Discussion, para 6) → simultaneous-contrast
- Kirschmann's Third Law (colour contrast also works at equiluminance) was supported by several earlier equiluminant studies using uniform surrounds (Discussion, para 1) → simultaneous-contrast
- Brightness contrast: with the uniform-luminance surround, the test shifted away from surround luminance: dY=-10 dB=-1.49 +/-0.76 cd/m2 (t[82]=-17.92); dY=-5 dB=-1.67 +/-0.83; dY=+10 dB=0.86 (Results, Brightness induction, Fig. 3) → simultaneous-contrast
- Chromatic surrounds induced brightness contrast in almost all conditions (Fig. 3 caption) → simultaneous-contrast

## Equiluminance
- Equiluminance for each observer was measured individually by minimally distinct border (MDB): adjust luminance of a coloured disc next to a grey disc (Y=20 cd/m2) until the border was minimal; 8 settings per colour, about 3 h over 3 days (Methods, Equiluminance measure) → equiluminance
- A residual chromatic border is always seen at MDB (Methods, Equiluminance measure) → equiluminance
- Result: at equiluminance no colour assimilation in any cardinal or diagonal condition; essentially no induction, except -l/0 which gave contrast (Results, para before "Brightness induction") → equiluminance
- At equiluminance the test ring was nonetheless seen darker than 20 cd/m2: dB=-0.84 (V=304, p=7.45e-10); stronger for diagonal (-1.66) than cardinal (-0.13) conditions; no brightness induction only in +s/0 and -s/0 (Results, Brightness induction) → equiluminance
- Equiluminant is not equal to equibright; authors propose future equibrightness experiments (Discussion, last para) → equiluminance
- Earlier equiluminant studies of colour induction exist, including with striped surrounds (Discussion, para 1) → equiluminance
