No book text, no page numbers were readable. The source gives topics only, not the content of the claims. Claims below say what the book covers, not what it states. Chapter numbers are those of the TOC listing.

- Ch. 1 covers how binders work and how different binder types look, and also colour stability and the artist's responsibility for permanence (TOC, ch. 1). The book therefore treats the binder as a distinct functional component of paint → paint-composition
- Ch. 3 (oil) covers the vehicle's function, drying oils, how oil colours are ground, oil absorption by pigments, pigment-oil reactions and stabilizers (TOC, ch. 3) → paint-composition
- Binders and media are treated per technique: acrylics (synthetic-resin and polymer colours, ch. 4), tempera emulsions (egg, oil, gum, wax, casein, ch. 5), and gums/casein/glues/waxes with preservatives for aqueous binders (ch. 12) (TOC) → paint-composition
- Ch. 2 (Pigments) opens with pigment basics: body colour vs. glaze, requirements for paint pigments, classifications, lakes and reduced pigments (TOC, ch. 2) → transparency-opacity, paint-composition
- Ch. 2 has a section on "mass tone and undertone" together with pigment nomenclature (TOC, ch. 2) → masstone-undertone, colour-index-names
- Ch. 2 includes an alphabetical pigment catalogue and a list of ASTM-approved pigments with specifications (TOC, ch. 2); contents not visible, so whether it cites Colour Index names is unverified → colour-index-names, lightfastness
- Ch. 2 covers permanence of pigments and variation of hues among tube colours; ch. 3 gives simple permanence rules for oil painting (TOC) → lightfastness
- Ch. 2 includes "permanent palettes of traditional pigments" for different techniques, restricted palettes, and colour as a technical tool (TOC, ch. 2) → limited-palette
- Ch. 2 also covers colour and light (optical effects) and pigment testing/refining (TOC, ch. 2) → transparency-opacity, lightfastness

Not covered (not readable): tinting-strength (not visible in the TOC); no actual definitions or figures for any concept.
Level: university/specialist reference for artists and conservators (the publisher calls it a standard reference). Only structure was accessible.
