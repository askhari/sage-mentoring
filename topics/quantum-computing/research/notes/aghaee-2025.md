Accessed: full text of the main paper in the local extraction, arXiv:2401.09549v4 (header date 2 Apr 2024; title "Interferometric Single-Shot Parity Measurement in InAs-Al Hybrid Devices", Microsoft Azure Quantum; about 9 pages of main text plus references). The supplement (Secs. S1-S9) was not in the text; claims that rest on it are cited only as the main text points to it. Page numbers are arXiv pages. The authors are Microsoft employees, so every claim below is "the authors, from Microsoft, report/interpret...". The device is a research prototype, not a qubit. Note: the extracted file is the April 2024 v4 text, which may differ from the published Nature 638 (2025) version.

## What was measured
- The authors report a single-shot, interferometric readout of fermion parity of a near-zero-energy state in a gate-defined InAs-Al nanowire. It is done by tunnel-coupling the nanowire to quantum dots (a triple-quantum-dot interferometer) and reading the quantum capacitance C_Q of one dot by rf reflectometry (abstract; p. 1, 2) → topological-qubits
- Quantum capacitance shifts depending on the parity state by up to about 1 fF. The simulated maximum is about 1 fF, close to the measured value (abstract; p. 5) → topological-qubits
- The signal is bimodal and h/2e-periodic in magnetic flux. The dot-nanowire loop shows a kurtosis oscillation of period 1.7(2) mT, matching the 1.7 mT expected for h/2e through the loop, measured on "device A" near B_parallel = 1.8 T (p. 3, Fig. 3) → topological-qubits
- Time traces show a random telegraph signal (switching between two C_Q values) wherever there is bimodality, and none where kurtosis is near zero (p. 4) → topological-qubits
- Readout quality: SNR of 4.9 in a 90 µs window, equivalent to SNR 1 in 3.7 µs (p. 4, Fig. 3; abstract). Raw data: about 1.5 x 10^4 consecutive 4.5 µs integrations, a 67 ms trace (p. 3) → topological-qubits
- Assignment error: the authors state a probability of assignment error of 1% at optimal measurement time. They credit the large capacitance shift and long poisoning time (abstract; p. 1) → topological-qubits
- Dwell time (the time before the parity flips): exponentially distributed with tau_RTS about 2.0(1) ms for both states in the aggregate of device A (Fig. 3i, p. 4). The abstract says "longer than 1 ms" at in-plane fields of about 2 T, and the Discussion repeats ">1 ms" (abstract; p. 8) → topological-qubits
- The authors identify the dwell time with the quasiparticle poisoning time tau_qpp (the time for a non-equilibrium quasiparticle to enter and flip the parity). A rate model gives about 2 ms for a non-equilibrium quasiparticle density of about 1 per µm^3 (p. 5) → topological-qubits
- A separate Cooper-pair-box experiment gives a quasiparticle density of about 0.6 per µm^3 at B = 0 (Sec. S9, cited on p. 5). The authors say it is within an order of magnitude of the density inferred from the dwell time and comparable to prior studies → topological-qubits
- Reproducibility: similar flux-dependent bimodality and telegraph signal on a second measurement of device A and on a device B (Sec. S6.1, cited on p. 5) → topological-qubits
- The method also gives the energy splitting E_M of the low-energy state with single-µeV resolution. The authors say this is hard to reach in transport measurements (pp. 1, 7) → topological-qubits

## Device and physics context
- Qubit concept in the authors' own words: information is stored in the fermion parity of Majorana zero modes (MZMs) at the ends of 1D topological superconductors (1DTS). Measurement-only topological computation reduces the operations to fusion, i.e. parity measurement (p. 1) → topological-qubits
- They claim non-Abelian anyon braiding and fusing give exponential suppression of errors from local noise, and that measurement-only schemes are a promising path to utility-scale machines needing millions of qubits (p. 1, citing refs 1-3 and 11-14). This is a motivation, not a result of this paper → topological-qubits
- Full qubit design: nanowire of 5 segments. Segments 2 and 4 (each about 3 µm) would be tuned topological, and the other three depleted. That gives two 1DTS with four MZMs and three interferometers. The experiment uses only the left topological section and its interferometer (p. 2) → topological-qubits
- The Majorana splitting scales as E_M ~ Delta_T exp(-L/xi), so the wire must be long compared with the coherence length. A longer wire also lowers the dot level spacing and charging energy and weakens the signal (p. 2). Dot 2 is 2.4 µm long with charging energy of about 45 µeV in operation (p. 2) → topological-qubits
- Material: 9.1 nm InAs quantum well, 60 nm wide, 6.5 nm thick Al strip (p. 1). Readout chain: JTWPA parametric amplifier plus HEMT, with shielding against infrared-generated quasiparticles (p. 2) → topological-qubits
- Model result: the signal depends on the total parity Z through |t_C|^2 = |t_L|^2 + |t_R|^2 + 2Z|t_L||t_R| sin(phi). The interferometer must be balanced (t_L ~ t_R) for a large capacitance difference (pp. 4-5, Eqs. 1-3) → topological-qubits
- With finite E_M the peaks become h/e-periodic and shifted between parity branches. The authors classify three observed regimes (a: h/2e, b: h/e zig-zag, c: bimodal with no flux dependence) and fit them with an MZM model with small to moderate E_M. They expect large E_M variation in current devices because of finite-size mesoscopic effects (pp. 6-8) → topological-qubits

## Topological gap protocol (TGP)
- The TGP is used only to choose the operating point (B_parallel about 1.8 T, V_WP1 = -1.832 V) for device A (p. 3). The authors say they tune "into the parameter regime where we expect the topological phase [56]" (p. 8) → topological-qubits
- The TGP itself is not described here, only cited: ref. 55 (Pikulin et al., arXiv:2103.12217, a protocol for a three-terminal device) and ref. 56 (Aghaee et al., Phys. Rev. B 107, 245423, 2023: "InAs-Al hybrid devices passing the topological gap protocol") (pp. 1, 10-11). The correlation between C_Q data and the TGP phase diagram is deferred to a follow-up paper "in preparation" (ref. 54, p. 1) → topological-qubits

## Claims and non-claims (strength)
- The paper does NOT claim to have created a topological qubit. It claims "significant progress towards" one (Discussion, p. 8). It calls the measurement "a step towards" fusion and a "necessary ingredient" of topological quantum computation (abstract; p. 1) → topological-qubits
- No qubit operation is shown: no coherent control, no braiding or fusion between two MZM pairs, no qubit coherence time, no error correction. Only the parity readout of one wire section is reported (my reading of the content of pp. 1-8; the paper never describes such operations as performed) → topological-qubits
- The wording on Majoranas is hedged. The data are called "consistent with" a parity measurement encoded in a pair of MZMs about 3 µm apart (abstract). The authors "interpret" the telegraph signal as parity switching and fit it to an MZM model with "good agreement" (pp. 4, 8) → topological-qubits
- Explicit disclaimer (p. 1): the measurement alone does not unequivocally separate MZMs in the topological phase from fine-tuned low-energy Andreev bound states in the trivial phase. It does require the state to be supported at both wire ends and weakly coupled to other low-energy states → topological-qubits
- Repeated in the Discussion (p. 8): the measurements "by themselves" do not determine whether the detected low-energy states are topological. The authors say that by fitting a trivial-Andreev-state model they tightly constrained such states' properties, and that the question is deferred to a separate publication → topological-qubits
- The quasi-MZM alternative (a "hidden" Majorana pair, Sec. S3, summarized p. 6): if the hidden modes are weakly coupled, the capacitance signal is strongly suppressed and inconsistent with the data. A coupling larger than the temperature is needed, which gaps them out. The authors say this is generically not weak-coupling and needs fine-tuning → topological-qubits
- Three control checks (p. 6): (1) injecting quasiparticles through a junction on another segment halves tau_RTS from 0 to 90 µV bias with Delta C_Q almost unchanged (device B, Fig. 5); (2) disconnecting the dots from the wire removes the telegraph signal, arguing against external two-level systems; (3) at 0.8 T, well inside the trivial phase, no h/e periodicity is seen → topological-qubits
- Outlook claim (p. 8): single-shot parity measurement is "a key requirement" for a Majorana-based architecture. The wording does not say it is sufficient → topological-qubits
- Stated limit on poisoning: the dwell time of about 2 ms is the time scale at which the parity is unprotected from quasiparticles in this device. The paper presents a long poisoning time as enabling good readout. It does not compare it with the gate or measurement times needed for computation → topological-qubits

No coverage of the controversy or history of earlier Majorana claims (the paper only cites Whiticar et al. 2020, ref. 45, as earlier interferometry progress).
