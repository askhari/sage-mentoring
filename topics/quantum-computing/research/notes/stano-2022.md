Page numbers are arXiv pages. Dates below are publication dates of the cited experiment, taken from Tables I-V. It is a data-collection ("Technical Review"), not a tutorial; the authors say they avoid judgments and outlooks (p. 2).
Caveat: the authors choose one "representative" value per experiment, which is subjective; they quote measured values only, with no extrapolations (p. 2). Notation: LD = spin-1/2 qubit, ST = singlet-triplet, HY = hybrid (includes exchange-only EO and resonant-exchange RX), /e electron, /h hole, /i impurity (donor).

## Scope and qubit types (spin-qubits)
- The review covers spin qubits in electrically gated semiconductor nanostructures. It excludes superconducting qubits, optically addressed impurities, self-assembled dots and nuclear (hyperfine) spin qubits. It does include charge qubits, because the same devices serve both. (p. 1)  → spin-qubits
- The stated reason for pursuing these qubits for scaling is their compatibility with industrial silicon fabrication. (p. 1)  → spin-qubits
- The field is varied in material, device design and encoding, which helps against roadblocks but makes comparison hard. This is the review's reason for common definitions. (p. 1)  → spin-qubits
- Encodings: the carrier can be a conduction electron, a valence hole or an atomic impurity (donor). The spin encoding is spin-1/2 ("LD"), singlet-triplet ("ST") or hybrid ("HY"), where hybrid means anything that is neither of the first two. (p. 2)  → spin-qubits
- Materials in the tables include GaAs/AlGaAs, Si/SiGe, Si/SiO2 (MOS), isotopically purified 28Si variants, Ge/SiGe, Ge/Si nanowires, Si:P donors and, in smaller number, InAs, InSb, carbon nanotubes and bilayer graphene. Host geometry is classed as 2D (e.g. 2DEG), 1D (nanowire, finFET) or zero-dimensional (implanted impurity). (pp. 2, 16-25)  → spin-qubits
- Exchange-based gates between two spins and between the singlet and triplet states of two electrons are physically the same process. Whether it counts as a 1-qubit or 2-qubit gate depends on the functionality implemented, so the boundary is blurry. (p. 8)  → spin-qubits
- Electric-dipole spin resonance (EDSR) works strongly for holes, with gate speeds up to GHz. It is less efficient for electron spin-1/2 qubits, where the highest speeds needed micromagnets (large field gradients) or materials with strong spin-orbit coupling such as InAs. (p. 8)  → spin-qubits
- Hybrid qubits can be tuned to resemble a charge qubit (very fast gates) or a spin qubit (slower, more coherent). The review calls this tunability their main advantage. (p. 8)  → spin-qubits
- Singlet-triplet oscillations can reach GHz frequencies. (p. 8)  → spin-qubits
- Charge qubits have much shorter coherence than spin qubits (T2* typically ps to tens of ns, T1 up to 45 µs). Their T1 is comparable to their coherence times, and echo techniques barely lengthen coherence. Nuclear spin noise is irrelevant for them. (p. 6, Table II, pp. 24-25)  → spin-qubits

## Coherence metrics: definitions (coherence-and-fidelity)
- T1 is the relaxation time (decay of qubit energy, "longitudinal"). T2 times describe decay of phase ("transverse"). The subscripts 1 and 2 come from the Bloch equations. (p. 3, App. A, p. 14)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- In terms of rates, 1/T1 = Γ_ex + Γ_rel, and 1/T2 = (Γ_ex + Γ_rel)/2 + Γ_φ, where Γ_φ is the pure-dephasing rate. (App. A, p. 14)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- T2* (inhomogeneous dephasing time) is measured with a Ramsey experiment. The qubit is prepared on the equator of the Bloch sphere and precesses freely for a variable time, and the in-plane polarization is then read out. The signal is a cosine with an envelope often fitted as a Gaussian, exp[-(t/T2*)^2]. (p. 3, Eq. 1)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- A frequently dominant cause of the Ramsey decay in spin qubits is fluctuating nuclear spins. Because nuclear dynamics are slow, spin-echo techniques from NMR can protect the coherence. (p. 3)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- T2 echo is measured with a Hahn echo: a pi rotation about an in-plane axis in the middle of the free evolution. Already in an early experiment (Ref. [290]) it extended coherence by about 100x. (p. 3)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Sequences with more than one echo pulse (e.g. CPMG) are grouped as "T2 DynD" (dynamical decoupling). T2 Rabi is the decay time of driven Rabi oscillations. Redfield's insight that driven and idling spins can decohere differently is cited. (p. 3)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Decay envelopes can be Gaussian (typical for T2*), exponential (typical for T1), or Lorentzian in the frequency domain. Tails are rarely resolvable, so the shape is hard to determine. Where it can be, the envelope shape hints at the noise source (e.g. nuclear spins vs charge impurities). (pp. 3-4, Fig. 1)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Operationally, a "coherence time" is the time for the fitted envelope to fall to a fraction (typically 1/e) of its initial value. It implies little about the functional form or the tails. (p. 5)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Slow Relaxation (T1) can be lengthened by isolating the dot, minimizing the dipole moment (weak phonon coupling), and lowering the transition energy (lower magnetic field). Seconds are reached and are "not of concern" for computing. T1 does matter for singlet-triplet readout, where triplet relaxation limits measurement fidelity. (p. 6)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- A coherence time or gate time alone says little. A fast qubit is not automatically good, and a very coherent qubit is less attractive if its gates are very slow. Hence the dimensionless quality factor and fidelity. (p. 7)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Gate (operation) time is defined here as T_op = 1/(2f), half the period of the signal, i.e. a pi rotation. (p. 8, Eq. 9)  → coherence-and-fidelity

## Fidelity, randomized benchmarking, SPAM, quality factor (coherence-and-fidelity)
- Fidelity of a state with a pure target is F = tr(rho |Psi><Psi|). Randomized benchmarking (RB) applies random Clifford gates ending with the inverting gate. The survival probability decays as F(m) ≈ A p^m + B, and the infidelity is 1-F = (1-p)(d-1)/d with d = 2^n. (pp. 9-10, Eqs. 13-16)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- RB extracts gate errors independently of measurement errors, even when the gate errors are orders of magnitude smaller, because A and B absorb state-preparation and measurement (SPAM) errors. (pp. 9-10)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Many papers convert Clifford fidelity to fidelity of "elementary" gates (pi/2 and pi rotations). The Clifford infidelity is then roughly 2-3x larger than the elementary-gate infidelity quoted in the tables. Cited ratios are 1.875 (1 qubit), 2.57 (2 qubits), and up to 9.75 in one case. The review itself calls this conversion "questionable". (p. 10, fn. 13)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Interleaved RB assigns a fidelity to one specific Clifford gate. (p. 10, fn. 11)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Measurement infidelity is defined from the off-diagonal probabilities. For a two-outcome readout it is (p01 + p10)/2. (p. 10, Eq. 19, Fig. 7)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Initialization fidelity is defined as the overlap of the actual prepared state with the target pure state. (p. 10)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- ⚠️ RB fidelity is not the error parameter in the fault-tolerance threshold theorem. The two can differ by orders of magnitude in the unfavorable direction (the RB infidelity can be small while the relevant error rate is much larger). So fidelity is only a rough indicator of progress toward the thresholds. (p. 9, fn. 8)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- The authors note that the entanglement fidelity is an alternative to average fidelity (relation (d+1)F = d·F_e + 1) and cite a reference advocating it, but they keep the average fidelity because most papers report it. (p. 10, fn. 12)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Quality factors: qubit Q = f_R · T2*, the number of operations before idle neighbouring qubits dephase. Gate Q = f_R · T2^Rabi, the number of discernible Rabi oscillations. Many papers include an extra factor of 2, so the review halves those values. In a toy model with exponential dephasing, 1-F ≈ 1/(4Q). Unitary (systematic) errors lower fidelity but not Q, so this is only a bound. (pp. 11-12, App. B pp. 14-15)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Reported quality factors mostly fall between a few and about 100, with a few approaching 1000. Table IV top value: 558 (gate Q, ST qubit, 28Si/SiGe, Aug 2024). (p. 12, Table IV, p. 35)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Charge-sensor detection of one elementary charge takes about 100 ns at typical sensitivity. This is the rough lower limit for spin measurement time, because spin is read out indirectly via spin-to-charge conversion (spin-dependent tunnelling or Pauli spin blockade). rf-sensors give the shortest times. Spin measurement times in the tables span about 50 ns to 0.2 s. (pp. 8-9, Fig. 6b, Table II-3/4)  → coherence-and-fidelity

## 1-qubit vs 2-qubit comparison (coherence-and-fidelity; key claim)
- The authors state that for gates, measurements and initializations alike, two-qubit infidelities stay at least one order of magnitude, usually more, above single-qubit infidelities. Less data exists for the two-qubit case. (p. 11, §IV.B)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Numbers (Table III, pp. 30-31) for spin-1/2 electron qubits in silicon (all dated by publication):
  - Best 1Q gate fidelity: 99.96 % (28Si/SiGe, Sep 2022), 99.957 % (28Si/SiO2, Apr 2019), 99.956 % (28Si/SiGe, Dec 2022).
  - Best 2Q gate fidelity: 99.81 % (CZ, 28Si/SiGe, Apr 2022), 99.65 % (CZ, 28Si/SiGe, Jan 2022), 99.51 % (CZ, 28Si/SiGe, Jan 2022), 99.48 % (28Si/SiGe, Jan 2024), 99.17 % (28Si/SiO2, Aug 2024).
  - Hence 1Q infidelity is about 4e-4, and the best 2Q infidelity about 1.9e-3 (roughly 5x larger; the same ranking also holds for most other entries).
  - Many 2Q entries are far lower: e.g. 92.72 % (Sep 2022), 83.1 % (iSWAP, Dec 2024).  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Holes in germanium: 1Q gate 99.992 % (Ge/SiGe, Jun 2023, Table III-2). The authors call it a new record above 99.99 % for any spin qubit. 2Q gate: 99.3 % (Ge/SiGe, Jul 2024), about two orders of magnitude of infidelity worse than the 1Q record (7e-3 vs 8e-5). (p. 11, Table III-2, p. 31)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Donor (impurity) qubits: 1Q 99.942 % (28Si:P, Oct 2016) and 99.78 % (Feb 2024), versus 2Q 90 % (Jul 2019) and 82 % (Sep 2024). (Table III-2, p. 31)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Hybrid (EO) qubit: 1Q 99.89 % (28Si/SiGe, Mar 2023), 2Q 96.3 % (same experiment; average 2Q Clifford fidelity 97.1 %). (Table III-1/2, pp. 30-31)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- ⚠️ Other 2Q gate fidelities in older/other platforms are far lower (e.g. 56-86 % for early Si electron CNOT/CROT, 72-90 % for GaAs singlet-triplet), so "best 2Q" is not representative of typical devices. (Table III-2, p. 31)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Gate times (Table II, pp. 26-28), the "slower 2Q gate" aspect: 1Q gates for electron spin-1/2 in Si are mostly 40 ns to about 1 µs (e.g. 70 ns in 28Si/SiGe, Apr 2022). 2Q gates for the same devices: 40 ns (28Si/SiGe, Apr 2022) and 42 ns (Dec 2024), more often 0.1-0.3 µs (and one at 3 µs). So 2Q gates are comparable to or somewhat slower than 1Q gates for electrons, not systematically much slower. The review makes no such explicit statement; this is read off the tables.  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Measurement fidelity: the "current record" is almost 99.99 % for an exchange-only (EO) qubit in purified Si (Ref. [26], Mar 2022; Table III-2 lists 99.975 %, which includes both preparation and measurement errors). Singlet-triplet readout with a "latched" Pauli-spin-blockade scheme gives above 99 %. ST in 28Si/SiO2 and 28Si/SiGe: 99.96 % (2024). Electron spin-1/2 readout: 99.56 % (28Si/SiGe, Dec 2022). Donor readout: 99.8 % (Mar 2017). Until recently measurement infidelity stayed above a few percent. (p. 11, Table III-2/3/4, pp. 31-33)  → coherence-and-fidelity
- Initialization: 99.81 % (donor, Sep 2022), 99.76 % (electron, 28Si/SiGe, Dec 2022); multi-qubit state prep (Bell/GHZ via algorithm) is lower, e.g. 96.6 % Bell (Apr 2022) and 3-qubit GHZ 76-88 %. (Table III-4/5, pp. 33-34)  → coherence-and-fidelity

## Record coherence times (spin-qubits, coherence-and-fidelity)
Values are from Table I (pp. 16-23) and the summary text (pp. 5-6). "Record" means largest table entry for the category.
- T1 (relaxation): longest is 57 s for a single electron in GaAs/AlGaAs (Aug 2018). Next are 43 s for a Si:P donor (Mar 2024) and 30 s for Si:P (Mar 2017). Authors note the record is "back" with a single electron in GaAs. T1 up to seconds exists in Si too (e.g. 9 s Si/SiO2 electron, Mar 2021; 5 s Si/SiGe, Apr 2019), but long T1 does not improve other figures of merit and mainly shows platform progress. (p. 6, Table I-1, p. 16)  → spin-qubits
- Hole T1: 1.2 ms to 32 ms in Ge/SiGe (2020-2021); 30 s was reported for a bilayer-graphene hole (Feb 2025, spin-valley relaxation). (Table I-1/2, pp. 16-17)  → spin-qubits
- T2* (inhomogeneous dephasing): largest is 2.4 ms for a 28Si:P donor qubit in the rotating frame (Oct 2016). In gated 28Si/SiO2 dots, 2 ms is for a dressed (driven) qubit (Sep 2022), and the undressed values are about 0.12 ms (several experiments, 2014-2021). Other values: 0.27 ms for 28Si:P (Oct 2014), 18 µs for a Ge/SiGe hole (May 2024), 10 µs for 28Si/SiGe (Nov 2019), 21 µs 28Si/SiGe (May 2020). GaAs singlet-triplet T2* is only about 10-100 ns. (Table I-2 to I-5, pp. 17-20)  → spin-qubits
- Reading of the pattern: T2* in the first GaAs experiments (about 2005) started near 10 ns, and the 2024 values span five orders of magnitude depending on qubit type, material and protection. Electron spin-1/2 qubits in purified silicon reach longer coherence than singlet-triplet qubits. Hole coherence has recently matched the best singlet-triplet qubits in almost every category. Impurity spins hold the record T2*, T2 echo and T2 Rabi. (p. 5)  → spin-qubits
- T2 echo (Hahn): largest is 9.2 ms for a 28Si:P donor in the rotating frame (Oct 2016). Other: 1.7 ms 28Si:P (Jul 2020), 1.2 ms for a gated electron in 28Si/SiO2 (Oct 2014), 1 ms 28Si/SiGe electron (Mar 2021), 0.14 ms 28Si/SiGe (Dec 2022), 70 µs for a Ge/SiGe hole (May 2024), 88 µs for a Si/SiO2 hole (Sep 2022). (Table I-5/6/7, pp. 20-22)  → spin-qubits
- T2 DynD (dynamical decoupling): 0.56 s for 28Si:P (Oct 2014); gated 28Si/SiO2 electron: 28 ms (Oct 2014 and Oct 2015); 28Si/SiGe electron: 3.1 ms (Dec 2017); Ge/SiGe hole: 1.9 ms (Jul 2024). A GaAs singlet-triplet qubit reached 0.87 ms (CPMG, Oct 2016); the text says "almost a millisecond". (Table I-7, p. 22; p. 3)  → spin-qubits
- T2 Rabi: largest is 0.18 ms for 28Si:P (Feb 2024), then 0.11 ms 28Si/SiGe electron (Dec 2017). (Table I-7, p. 22)  → spin-qubits
- Isotopic purification of Si (28Si) removes nuclear spin noise and is central to the longest electron coherence. In this review, "using silicon, natural and purified, was crucial" for the highest single-qubit fidelities. (p. 11)  → spin-qubits

## Gate speeds across qubit types (spin-qubits)
- Fastest 1Q gates are for charge qubits, below 0.1 ns, and the hybrid qubits can match this (picoseconds range). They are limited by electronics rather than the qubit. (p. 8, Table II-1/2, p. 26)  → spin-qubits
- Spin-qubit 1Q gate times (Table II-1/2/3, pp. 26-28):
  - singlet-triplet: 0.2 ns (GaAs, 2014);
  - electron spin-1/2: 4.1 ns (GaAs, Dec 2014), 4.8 ns (InSb, 2013), 40-100 ns in Si, about 1 µs in many 28Si/SiO2 devices;
  - hole spin: 0.41 ns (Ge/Si nanowire, May 2023), 0.92 ns (Jan 2022), 8.8 ns (Ge/SiGe, Jul 2020);
  - donors: 0.15 to a few µs, up to 0.16 ms for a nuclear-spin-related qubit.  → spin-qubits
- Spin-qubit 2Q gate times: 0.80 ns (donor, Jul 2019), 2 ns (ST, GaAs, 2019), 9 ns (Ge/SiGe hole, Mar 2021), 40 ns (28Si/SiGe electron, Apr 2022), 42 ns (Dec 2024), 24 ns (Si/SiO2 hole, May 2024). (Table II-3, p. 28)  → spin-qubits
- A fast gate is not a good qubit unless compared with coherence time. The review therefore reports Q. (p. 7)  → spin-qubits

## Size of arrays and scaling (spin-qubits, hardware-landscape)
- Scaling up to many qubits is called "the biggest current challenge" of the field and applies to all qubit platforms, not only semiconductors. Large 2D arrays seem necessary because fault-tolerance thresholds for 1D arrays remain too low (Ref. [80]). (p. 12)  → hardware-landscape
- The review defines three functionality levels: an N-qubit "device" (gating and charge sensing work, but quantum operation is limited), "simulator" (tunable, connected, stable, with spin-spin correlations measurable) and "processor" (two-axis control, readout, tunable pairwise interaction, can run N-qubit algorithms). The assignment is the most subjective in the paper. (pp. 12-13)  → spin-qubits
- Making a double dot into a first fully-functional processor took about 10-15 years (first experiments about 2005). The first "processor" beyond a double dot was reached in 2021: electrons in silicon (Ref. [349]) and holes in germanium (Ref. [136], both Mar-Jun 2021 in Table III). Refs. [375, 396, 422] were also recognised as "processor" level, with fidelity high enough for elementary circuits only in the latest one. (p. 13, Fig. 10)  → spin-qubits
- Largest arrays at device level (Table V, pp. 36-37): 4x4 Ge/SiGe holes (Aug 2023), 12-qubit Si/SiO2 devices (May 2024 and Oct 2024), 10 (Ge/SiGe, Jul 2024), 9 (Si/SiGe, 2016 and 2019). Only 3-qubit processor-level operation, e.g. 3-qubit GHZ states and a Toffoli gate (Table II-3, p. 28; Table III-5, p. 34). The first 3-qubit gate was used in an error-correction demonstration (Ref. [350], Aug 2022). The Table V simulator entries (4 qubits, 2019) are for 28Si/SiGe and GaAs. The text notes the authors see "excellent reasons for optimism" because recent results come from many groups, materials and geometries. (pp. 7, 13, 36-37)  → spin-qubits
- Cryogenic temperature: some key results are quoted at higher temperature (1 K or 1.5 K) for Si/SiO2 and Ge devices (table footnotes, pp. 17, 30-31). One experiment used on-chip cryo-CMOS control electronics (Table I-3 footnote, p. 18). Other temperature values are given only in footnotes, so there is no general statement.  → spin-qubits

## Cross-platform context (hardware-landscape)
- The review covers only spin qubits and gives no comparison with superconducting, ion or atom platforms. The only cross-platform statements are that scaling is the field's biggest challenge for all platforms (p. 12) and that RB fidelity should be treated with caution relative to threshold requirements (p. 9). Its tables allow a spin-qubit-only snapshot (above) to be placed against other sources' numbers.  → hardware-landscape
- It reports a surge of progress in recent years, in both fidelity and array size, from many groups across materials and geometries. (pp. 11, 13)  → hardware-landscape
